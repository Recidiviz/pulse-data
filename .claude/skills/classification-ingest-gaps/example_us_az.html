US_AZ · Classification diagnosis

What Arizona needs to ingest for the classification metrics

Every metric on the Initial Diagnosis tab of Looker dashboard 319, plus the score-vs-violence views (#110752) and the state-agnostic violence model (#110836), measured against Arizona's data in staging on 2026-11-03. Arizona currently appears in none of them.

Three ingest changes unlock everything

1. Mark custody classifications approved

Set assessment_decision = APPROVED on AZ_CUSTODY_CLASS assessments. All 1,401,071 have no decision today, so approved_assessment_events drops every one. AZ records no approval step, so this follows the MI and IX pattern of marking every assessment approved.

Unlocks: every metric on this page.

2. Flag violent incidents

Set is_violent on state_incarceration_incident. It is empty on all 190,512 AZ incidents. The definition is AZ's to make: it becomes the outcome the model predicts and the safety metric a launch is judged on. See the section below.

Unlocks: violent incident rates, ROC/AUC, and the violence model.

3. Map overrides and reclassifications

Map assessment_override_level from IS_OVERRIDE and ASSIGNED_CUSTODY_LEVEL_DESC, and assessment_subtype from IS_RECLASSIFICATION. Both already sit in assessment_metadata, so this is a mapping change with no new raw data.

Unlocks: the four override tiles, and initial vs. reclassification breakdowns.

Defining violence: Arizona decides

is_violent should mark the incidents AZ cares about most, because everything downstream rests on it: it is the outcome the violence model is fit to, the yardstick for the current score's AUC, and the safety metric a classification launch is monitored on. We can start from a provisional definition to get the pipeline running, but AZ should confirm or replace it before any result is shown as final.

Earlier states combined a severity class with a list of violent incident types rather than using severity alone:

StateViolent incident definition
US_TNClass A incidents that are an assault, homicide, hostage-taking, or sexual assault.
US_MIClass I or II misconducts (the two most severe classes) that are an assault, homicide, sexual assault, riot incitement, or felony.
US_IXDOR Class A incidents.

Severity alone doesn't work for AZ. Of the 10,954 Class A (HIGHEST) incidents since 2023, most are contraband: promoting contraband (3,727), possessing a weapon (2,685), and possessing a communication device (1,554). Most assaults sit in Class B instead: fighting (9,379), assault on an inmate (3,447), and assault on staff without serious injury (1,495).

Candidate starting definitionIncident typesIncidents since 2023
Narrow: Class A violence Aggravated assault (inmate on inmate) 789, assault on staff with serious injury 249, participation in a riot 206, arson 127, murder (1st and 2nd degree) 8 1,379
Broad: Class A or B violence Narrow, plus Class B fighting 9,379, assault on an inmate 3,447, assault on staff without serious injury 1,495, sexual contact 386, assault on staff by throwing substances 287, rioting 37 16,410

The broad definition matches how TN and MI combined their two most severe classes and gives the model about 12 times as many outcomes to learn from; the narrow one tracks only serious harm. Either is a reasonable provisional choice until AZ weighs in.

Questions for AZ

  • Which incidents does AZ most want classification to prevent: serious injury only, any assault, or also fights?
  • Should Class B fighting count? It is the single largest violent type (9,379) and drives the broad definition.
  • Should threats and intimidation (2,521 Class B; 131 Class A for gang activity), weapon possession, or riot participation count as violence or as other serious misconduct?
  • Should assault on staff by throwing substances count?
  • Do any non-violent Class A incidents belong, for example attempts or conspiracies to commit a Class A offense when the target offense was violent?

Things to know before mapping

  • Level scales differ. AZ's scored assessment_level and ASSIGNED_CUSTODY_LEVEL_DESC use different scales: 4,254 classifications in 2025–26 have a HIGH scored level, an assigned level of CLOSE, and IS_OVERRIDE FALSE. Use IS_OVERRIDE to decide what is an override; don't infer it by comparing the two fields until the scales are reconciled.
  • Overrides are common. Of 78,018 custody classifications since January 2025, 23,491 (30%) are flagged IS_OVERRIDE. The most common is a MAXIMUM score assigned to CLOSE (11,538).
  • Question scores are already ingested. assessment_metadata carries every question's score (AGE_SCORE, DSCPLN_VIO_SCORE, ESCAPE_HISTORY_SCORE, GANG_AFF_SCORE, MST_SERIOUS_SCORE, PR_MST_SERIOUS_SCORE, PROGRAM_SCORE). Once is_violent lands, the playbook's "reweight the current questions" analysis needs no further ingest.
  • Other AZ RISK instruments stay out. AZ_INTERNAL_RISK_CLASS, AZ_GEN_RISK_LVL, AZ_VLNC_RISK_LVL and CCRRA would also enter the views if marked approved. Mark only the instruments that should be diagnosed.
